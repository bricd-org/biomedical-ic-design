Three things to try

  1. Let the artefact choose the reference. At the start (step size μ 0.010, L 8, impedance share 60 %, Figure 31.1's settings) the readout shows accel. alone 6.1 dB, impedance alone 5.1 dB and both 8.7 dB, in the same order as the figure. Slide the impedance share of the artefact to 100 %: impedance alone climbs to 11.8 dB and accel. alone falls to 2.1 dB, and impedance alone now beats both (10.2 dB), because the accelerometer's weights only add jitter. At 0 % the two trade places: accel. alone 6.8 dB, impedance alone 3.5 dB.
  2. Give each reference the taps it needs. Back at 60 %, set taps per reference L to 1: accel. alone drops to 3.2 dB while impedance alone keeps 5.2 dB. At L = 16 accel. alone rises to 9.8 dB and both to 12.2 dB, while impedance alone stays near 5 dB: the accelerometer's effect lags through the mechanics of the skin and needs a longer filter, while the impedance trace needs only a few taps.
  3. Switch off the regularisation. Back at L = 8, click regularised (δ ≈ reference power) to turn it off: artefact removed falls from 8.7 dB to −11.8 dB, and the filter now adds far more error than it removes (other recordings land between about −14 and +3 dB). Between movements the references are nearly zero, and dividing by their power lets the EEG throw the weights about.

Check yourselfWith both references, slide step size μ all the way to the right: artefact removed rises to a peak of about 10.4 dB near μ = 0.05 and falls to 8.3 dB at 0.20. Why does a larger step eventually remove less?
