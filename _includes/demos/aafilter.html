Three things to try

  1. Drop to second order. From the start (2 kS/s, corner at 600 Hz, interferer at 1.5 kHz), set Butterworth order to 2: the attenuation at the interferer falls from 31.8 dB to 16.0 dB, well short of the 30 dB the EMG path needs. Each order is worth about 8 dB here, 6 dB per octave over the 1.3 octaves from 600 Hz to 1.5 kHz.
  2. Follow the chopper ripple. Set Butterworth order back to 4 and interferer frequency to 4 kHz: it aliases to 0 Hz, after the filter has cut it by 65.9 dB (66 dB in the chapter). Because the chopper and SAR clocks are locked, the ripple lands at DC as an offset that the high-pass removes, not as a tone in the band.
  3. Sample faster, filter less. Set sample rate fs to 4 kS/s, Butterworth order to 2 and interferer frequency to 3.5 kHz: it now folds to the band edge (490–510 Hz), and second order already gives 31 dB there. Doubling the rate moved the first folding frequency, fs − 500 Hz, from 1.5 to 3.5 kHz and halved the filter order.
