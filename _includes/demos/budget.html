Three things to try

  1. Swap the electrodes. At the start (wet electrodes, LNA current 2.00 µA) the channel shows 0.51 µVrms and 6.8 µW, and the electrodes take 44 % of the noise power. Click dry electrodes: the noise rises to 1.26 µVrms, still inside the 1.5 µVrms target, and the electrodes now take 91 % of it (Worked Example 29.1).
  2. Throw current at the dry channel. With dry electrodes, raise the LNA current from 2.00 to 8.00 µA. The noise falls only from 1.26 to 1.22 µVrms, while the power doubles to 14.0 µW and the readout says the channel misses the power target.
  3. Let the converter pay for the LNA. Back on wet electrodes at 2.00 µA, click EMG · SAR: the power falls from 6.8 to 4.9 µW, because a small share of the SAR replaces the ΔΣ converter's 1.9 µW. In ECG/EEG · ΔΣ the LNA current can rise to 4.50 µA (9.8 µW) before the power target is missed; in EMG · SAR, to 6.25 µA (10.0 µW). By then the electrodes, not the LNA, are the largest noise term even with wet electrodes.
