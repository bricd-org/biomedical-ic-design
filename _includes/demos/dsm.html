Three things to try

  1. Mind the model gap. At the start (2nd order, OSR 128, −6 dBFS), in-band SQNR, simulated reads 84.9 dB, the value in Table 19.1, against 100.2 dB for the linear model at full scale. The −6 dBFS back-off accounts for 6 dB; the other 9 dB come from the 1-bit quantiser, whose larger error and effective gain of about 0.63 the model ignores (Worked Example 19.1). Raise the input to −1 dBFS and the simulation stays at 84.9 dB: near full scale the in-band noise grows as fast as the signal.
  2. Drop to first order. Click 1st order: the simulated SQNR falls to 55.5 dB, again as in Table 19.1, and the model to 65.8 dB. The gold model line now rises at 20 dB per decade instead of 40, so far more of the shaped noise is left inside the shaded band.
  3. Find the idle tones. Still at 1st order, set oversampling ratio OSR to 64 and input amplitude to −30 dBFS: the SQNR falls to 18.5 dB, and the spectrum shows why: the loop's regular bit pattern puts a cluster of tall spurs inside the shaded band, near its edge. Click 2nd order and the same settings give 48.6 dB.
