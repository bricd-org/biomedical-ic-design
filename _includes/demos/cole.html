Three things to try

  1. Walk along the arc. At the start (tetrapolar, fc 30 kHz, excitation 50 kHz) the tissue reads 42.3 Ω, −12.0°, the chapter's 42 Ω at −12°. Slide excitation frequency down to 1 kHz: 58.7 Ω, close to R0, as the current flows around the cells; up to 1 MHz: 31.4 Ω, close to R∞, as it passes through the membranes too.
  2. Measure with two electrodes. Back at 50 kHz, click bipolar, wet: measured jumps to 2041 Ω at −0.2°, the tissue's 42 Ω plus about 2 kΩ of electrodes; bipolar, dry reads 10041 Ω. The I output grows from 414 µV to 20.4 mV while Q stays at 88 µV: the tissue is now 2 % of the reading, which is why the tetrapolar pair exists.
  3. Change the spread of cells. Back on tetrapolar, slide dispersion α from 0.70 to 1.00: the arc rounds out into a semicircle and the tissue reads 40.2 Ω at −19.2°. Lower it to 0.50 and the arc flattens, its centre sinking below the real axis: 43.2 Ω at −8.1°, as a wider spread of cell sizes smears the transition over more frequencies.
