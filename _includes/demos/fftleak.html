Three things to try

  1. Break coherence. At the start (67.00 cycles, 12 bits, no window) the SNR read from the FFT is 73.9 dB against the ideal 74.0 dB. Slide the tone to 67.30 cycles: the record is not coherent, the leakage skirt fills the spectrum and the same perfect converter reads 10.2 dB.
  2. Window the leak. Keep 67.30 cycles and choose Hann: 36.3 dB, much better, but the Hann sidelobes close to the tone still count as noise. Choose Blackman–Harris: 73.8 dB, within 0.2 dB of ideal, because its leakage stays below the 12-bit floor outside the main lobe.
  3. Avoid an even cycle count. Back on no window, set the tone to 64.00 cycles: coherent, yet the SNR reads 75.7 dB, better than an ideal 12-bit converter can be. With exactly 64 samples per cycle the same 64 codes repeat, so the quantisation error collapses into a handful of spurs instead of noise; a prime count such as 61 or 67 reads 73.9 dB.

Check yourselfBlackman–Harris restores the ideal SNR at 67.30 cycles with 12 bits. Keep that window and tone and test a 16-bit converter instead: what do you expect?
