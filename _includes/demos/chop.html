Three things to try

  1. Switch the chopper off. From the start, click chopper on so that it reads chopper off: noise 0.5–100 Hz jumps from 0.272 µVrms to 1.31 µVrms, nearly all of it flicker noise, and the full 0.39 mV offset now sits on the signal (Worked Example 9.1).
  2. Chop a small input pair. Turn the chopper back on and raise amplifier 1/f corner fc to 4.7 kHz, close to the 4.6 kHz of a 100 µm² pair: fch is now below the corner, the caption warns in red, and the noise reads 0.367 µVrms, 40 % above the 0.26 µVrms white floor. Raise chopper frequency fch to 10.2 kHz and it falls to 0.306 µVrms, within 20 % (Worked Example 9.1).
  3. Count the cost of a fast clock. With fch back at 4 kHz, click dry electrodes: the residual offset 2fchΔq·Rs rises from 0.40 µV to 7.9 µV, because Rs grows from 50 kΩ to 1 MΩ. Raise fch to 10.2 kHz and it reaches 20.4 µV: chop just above the 1/f corner, not as fast as you can.
