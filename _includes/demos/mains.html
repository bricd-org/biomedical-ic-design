Three things to try

  1. Switch off the right-leg drive. From the start the residual is 58 µVpp. Drag right-leg drive loop gain to off: the full 28.3 Vpp of the CMRR test (Worked Example 4.3) reaches the electrodes, the residual grows 103-fold, one plus the loop gain, to 5.99 mVpp, and the demo reports that it fails the test.
  2. Find the input impedance the test needs. Keep the right-leg drive off and raise common-mode input impedance Zcm. The test first passes at 1.7 GΩ (969 µVpp); at 1.9 GΩ the residual is 881 µVpp, the 90 dB of Worked Example 4.4.
  3. Let the amplifier take over. Still without the right-leg drive, push Zcm to its 10 GΩ maximum: the residual falls to 397 µVpp and the caption now blames the amplifier's own CMRR. Lower amplifier CMRR to 89 dB, the bare minimum of Worked Example 4.3, and the test fails at 1.12 mVpp, because the imbalance term still adds its share.

Check yourselfWith the right-leg drive off and Zcm at 200 MΩ, the residual is 5.99 mVpp. What does raising amplifier CMRR from 100 dB to 130 dB do to it?
