Three things to try

  1. Count the bits per doubling. At the start (2nd order + CoI, M = 1024) the worst error is 1.04e-5, 16.5 bits at 500 S/s, the figure Chapter 19 derives. Slide cycles per conversion M to 2048: 18.6 bits, two bits more for twice the cycles, because the error falls as 1/M2.
  2. Read the loop with a counter. Set M back to 1024 and click 1st order + counter: the worst error jumps to 1.74e-3, only 9.2 bits, and M = 2048 buys just one bit more (10.2 bits). At one bit per doubling, 16 bits would take 65 536 cycles per conversion.
  3. Trade rate for resolution. Back on 2nd order + CoI, set M = 512 to double the output rate to 1000 S/s: the gold worst-case line rises above the green 16-bit line, at 14.5 bits. With the clock fixed at 512 kHz, M = 1024 is the shortest conversion on the slider that clears 16 bits.
